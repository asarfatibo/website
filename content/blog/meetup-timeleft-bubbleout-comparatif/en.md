Meetup, Timeleft and bubbleOut solve different problems: Meetup plugs you into interest groups that have been running for years, Timeleft seats you at a table with five strangers on a Wednesday night, bubbleOut gets you into local Clubs and free Events you can come back to next week. The real question isn't which one is best, it's what you want to still have the next morning.

This comparison covers the format, the price and the community layer of each one, including what each does better than the other two.

## Which app should you use to meet people in Montreal or Paris?

Pick Meetup if you want an established group around a specific interest, Timeleft if organizing anything is what stops you, bubbleOut if you want to see the same people again without paying.

## How does Meetup work, and who is it actually for?

Meetup lets you search groups by interest and city, then sign up for their meetups. Joining is free, and Meetup states plainly that membership costs nothing on the attendee side.

It's the oldest of the three, and that shows in a good way. In Montreal and Paris you'll find hiking, board game and language groups that have been running for a decade, with organizers who know what they're doing and a rhythm that's already set. For a niche interest, Meetup is often the only place where something already exists.

Two caveats. First, the tone: plenty of groups carry a professional networking feel that lands badly with people in their twenties. Second, the organizer cost. Running a group requires a paid subscription, and Meetup notes that the rate depends on your country, currency and the platform you signed up through. Starting your own group becomes a budget decision, which quietly filters out a lot of people.

## How does Timeleft work, and what does it cost?

Timeleft puts you in a small group of strangers for a dinner, drinks or a run, with nothing for you to organize. You answer a few questions, the app assembles the group by age range, personality and language, and you find out who's coming shortly before it starts.

The scale is real: Timeleft claims more than 3 million members, over 200 cities across 52 countries, more than 80,000 dinners attended and 720 bookings a day. The mechanic deserves credit because it removes the actual blocker. Nobody to convince, no plan to carry, you just show up. If proposing an outing is the thing you never do, this is an effective way in.

On price, be careful with what's floating around online, because the model has shifted. The official site now says you pay for your food and drinks at the venue. Check the exact cost in the app for your city before booking, since it varies by country and restaurant.

The limit is structural. It's a one-off. The group exists for one evening, then it dissolves. If you click with two people, following up is entirely on you, with no shared space to find everyone again. A lot of people do dinner after dinner without ever building a circle.

## What does bubbleOut do differently?

bubbleOut splits two things the others blend together: the outing and the group. An Event is a specific plan, posted by a member in under two minutes. A Club is the community around an interest, with its members, its chat and its upcoming outings.

In practice, the [Clubs and Events around you in Montreal](/en/montreal) are created by members themselves. As of July 12, 2026, 20 active Clubs hold 272 cumulative unique members, which means people join more than one. The two largest are [Time to apéritz](https://bubbleout.app.link/) with 116 members and [Club Run MTL](https://bubbleout.app.link/) with 71. On the outings side, 240 Events have happened since October 2025, and attendees rate them 4.2 out of 5 on average (46 ratings).

Inside a Club, Talks let any member pitch an outing idea and the group vote on it. That's what replaces the single organizer: the next plan comes from the group instead of one person burning out.

The network is 2,170 people across Montreal and Paris. That's small next to Timeleft's 3 million, and it's deliberate: discovery is capped at 100 km, so everything you see is genuinely near you. If you're in Paris, [the launch is underway](/en/paris) and the first Clubs are being founded right now.

Where bubbleOut is weaker: for a very specific interest, a ten-year-old Meetup group will have more people. And if you want zero decisions to make, Timeleft's automatic grouping asks less of you.

## FAQ

### Is Meetup free?

Free to attend, yes. Meetup states that membership costs nothing: you can sign up, join groups and go to meetups without paying. Creating and running your own group does require a paid organizer subscription, and the rate depends on your country and the platform you subscribe through.

### How much does Timeleft cost?

Timeleft's official site currently says you pay for your food and drinks directly at the venue. The model changed recently, so prices quoted in older articles are no longer reliable. Check the amount shown in the app for your city before booking.

### What's the best Meetup alternative in Montreal?

It depends on what's missing for you. If it's the organizer cost, bubbleOut lets you create a Club or an Event for free. If it's the networking tone, bubbleOut and Timeleft skew younger. If it's that no group covers your interest, no app replaces starting one yourself.

### Are these dating apps?

No, none of the three. Meetup runs interest groups, Timeleft assembles tables of strangers, bubbleOut connects people around activities and Clubs. There's no swiping and no couple logic on bubbleOut. The [FAQ](/en/faq) covers how it works in detail.

### Can you use more than one of these at once?

Yes, and in the first few months that's usually the smart move. A Timeleft dinner to get started, a Meetup group for a specific interest, a bubbleOut Club to build a regular rhythm. All three are free to try on the attendee side.
